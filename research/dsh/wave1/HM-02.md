# HM-02

Prescription safety checker for Indian brand medicines: resolve brand names and combinations to active ingredients and strengths, flag interactions, duplicate ingredients and excessive doses by age/weight/kidney function, reduce alert fatigue. Test set needs a pharmacist.

## Mind-boggling demo angles (3)
- Brand X-ray: select two Indian brands and watch each expand into its ingredients and strengths, revealing an overlapping ingredient hidden inside a combination product. A proposed browser-local version would use a pharmacist-checked catalogue subset and Tesseract.js for optional pack-photo input, with mandatory correction before checking.
- Patient-context replay: change age, weight or kidney-function inputs and see which pharmacist-approved dose rules change, with the calculation and missing-information checks shown beside each alert. Run a small, explicitly reviewed ruleset locally in JavaScript or through a browser-connected rules service—not an LLM-generated dosing recommendation.
- Signal, not sirens: load a synthetic multi-medicine prescription and switch between raw alerts and grouped, evidence-linked alerts, while keeping serious warnings visible. The browser groups repeated warnings by ingredient or mechanism and lets a pharmacist record overrides; the demonstration shows the interface concept, not proven alert-fatigue reduction.

## Risks (3)
- Clinical false reassurance: the shortlisted components do not establish complete Indian-brand coverage or validated age-, weight- and kidney-specific dosing. OCR, combination parsing and incomplete interaction coverage can miss hazards; unresolved medicines must be marked unknown, and pharmacist-reviewed test cases are essential.
- Data rights and freshness: DDInter is accessible but its download page states All Rights Reserved; an open redistribution license was not established. The Indian dataset advertises MIT, but upstream rights, current formulations and clinical accuracy still need assessment before distribution or deployment.
- Privacy and external-service exposure: MedRecon's supplied architecture uses Gemini and a public FHIR test server. Use synthetic cases for judging, do not send identifiable prescriptions to public services, and separate browser-local OCR from any explicitly disclosed backend processing.

## Missing pieces (4)
- A pharmacist-maintained Indian brand-to-ingredient-and-strength layer with formulation, release type, salt equivalence, liquid concentrations, multi-ingredient combinations and explicit handling of uncertain matches. The examined catalogue's two composition fields are a starting point, not proof of complete normalization.
- A validated patient-specific dosing knowledge base covering pediatric and adult limits, frequency, total daily exposure and renal adjustment, including which renal-function measure each rule requires. The examined projects do not establish this complete coverage.
- A pharmacist-labelled gold-standard test set covering dangerous and harmless prescriptions, duplicate ingredients, ambiguous brands, boundary doses and missing patient information, with measured false-negative and false-positive rates.
- An integrated alert policy and workflow: evidence provenance, severity prioritization, duplicate-warning grouping, override reasons and usability testing. None of the examined components establishes that the proposed alert-fatigue reduction is clinically effective.
