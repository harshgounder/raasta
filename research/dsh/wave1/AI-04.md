## Mind-boggling demo angles (3)
- Failure flight recorder: use browser toggles to remove the relevant retrieved chunk or inject an unsupported answer claim. A Python evaluator updates separate retrieval and faithfulness panels, with highlighted evidence and a probable—not definitive—failure diagnosis.
- Turn a document into its own exam: upload a document in the browser, generate synthetic questions through Ragas, and run two retrieval configurations against the same questions. One screen shows which configuration recovered evidence and whether the generator actually used it.
- Disconnect-the-internet demonstration: after caching assets, run MiniLM embeddings, a small document search, and the quantized Qwen generator inside the browser. Display local reference-based retrieval checks alongside clearly labeled precomputed judge scores; do not present those scores as live offline hallucination detection.

## Risks (3)
- Misdiagnosis: faithfulness measures support in retrieved context, not independent factual truth. Missing references, imperfect synthetic questions, and judge errors can mislabel retrieval failures as hallucinations; use labeled examples and treat diagnoses as probabilistic.
- Latency and cost: RAGChecker's documented example uses a remote 70B model, while the small browser generator alone downloads 512MB of weights. Use small test batches, cached assets, and asynchronous progress; do not assume a small local generator is a reliable evaluator.
- Data governance: remote evaluation can expose uploaded documents and answers to model providers. Keep credentials server-side, obtain consent or use local processing, preserve dataset attribution, and resolve the ONNX conversion's unspecified license before redistributing it.

## Missing pieces (3)
- No reviewed project was verified to supply the entire requested workflow turnkey. A unified screen still needs to join synthetic questions, retrieved evidence, generated answers, separate scores, and diagnosis.
- A shared result schema and diagnostic decision layer are needed to distinguish missing evidence, failure to use available evidence, unsupported claims, and unanswerable questions; metric scores alone do not establish causality.
- A validated, fully client-side evaluation stack is missing from the reviewed options. Browser embeddings and generation are feasible, but synthetic-question quality, evaluator calibration, offline packaging, and cross-browser performance still require implementation and testing.
