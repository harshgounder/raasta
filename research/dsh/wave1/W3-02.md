## Mind-boggling demo angles (3)
- Proposed demo: “Steal my agent key.” Reveal a disposable testnet session key in the browser, then let the judge attempt a forbidden contract, selector or recipient; display validation failures alongside a successful permitted payment, proving that the browser UI is not the security boundary.
- Proposed demo: “Death by a thousand small payments.” Configure a 5-USDC per-transaction cap and 12-USDC daily budget, then launch repeated 5-USDC payments from the browser: the third must fail despite passing the per-transaction rule. Drive a local development chain from the UI to demonstrate the specified daily-reset boundary after implementing and testing cumulative accounting.
- Proposed demo: “An agent that must ask.” An optional browser-local model proposes an out-of-policy payment, which remains blocked until the owner signs that exact transaction through a separate approval flow. Show that this one-off approval does not broaden the agent's session, then demonstrate expiry or revocation in the same dashboard.

## Risks (3)
- Permission escape through configuration: Rhinestone documents permissive defaults and an unmatched-call Orchestrator fallback; Alchemy warns that global validation can upgrade the account or change ownership. The agent must not receive these broader capabilities, and restrictions must survive direct submission outside the UI.
- A leaked key can still spend its legitimate allowance. Contract bugs, incomplete cumulative accounting or unexamined execution paths can invalidate the intended boundary; revocation also races attacker transactions until mined. The reviewed material does not establish an unconditional malicious-agent security guarantee for the complete proposed configuration.
- Fork/deployment obligations: Smart Sessions and Alchemy use copyleft licenses, unlike MIT-licensed Kernel. Review redistribution and applicable source-disclosure obligations, and pin compatible deployed contracts, SDKs, EntryPoint and bundler infrastructure before relying on a live demo.

## Missing pieces (3)
- No reviewed project was verified to supply the complete requested browser workflow: policy editor, owner/session separation, adversarial transaction controls, receipts and exact-transaction owner approvals in one turnkey application.
- A verified recurring daily monetary budget remains a gap. Retrieved documentation establishes spending, timeframe and usage/rate primitives, but not the complete daily-reset and cross-session aggregation semantics required here; transaction-count limits are not monetary budgets.
- The owner-exception path needs explicit integration: an out-of-policy request must remain blocked until a separate owner authorization approves the exact transaction, without silently widening the agent's permissions. Recipient argument restrictions and all relevant execution paths also need regression coverage.
