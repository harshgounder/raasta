Clause 6.7 Pedestrian Crossings, "Painted Zebra Crossing" sub-heading (unnumbered), Fig. 48 | "The kerb ramp slope should not be steeper than 1:15 and slope on flared sides should be 1:10 as shown in Fig. 48." | CONFIRMED | Other 1:15 uses in the same doc: side ramps at property entrances 1:15 (cl. 6.2, Fig. 16/17); on-street accessible parking ramp 1:15 (cl. 6.11). Vehicle access ramps are 1:8. Sub-clause has no number, only the parent 6.7. Filename on law.resource.org says "078.2.2020" but the content is IRC:103-2022. |
| 1b | IRC:103-2012 kerb ramp max gradient (comparison) | https://law.resource.org/pub/in/bis/irc/irc.gov.in.103.2012.pdf, cl. 6.2.3 Kerb ramps; also cl. 6.7.2 Key guidelines, cl. 6.8.1 Ramp | 6.2.3: "Standard kerb ramps are cut back into the footpath (flush with roadway), at a gradient not greater than 1:12 (Fig. 10, 11 & 12)". 6.7.2: "The gradient of the ramp should ideally be 1:20, and not to exceed 1:12 in any case." 6.8.1: "Slope of ramp is to be a maximum of 1:12." | CONFIRMED (for the 2012 comparison) | So 2012 = 1:12 and 2022 = 1:15 for kerb ramps. The 2022 revision is gentler. Do not cite 1:12 as "IRC:103" without the year. 2012 ramps in general (cl. 6.8.1, Table 6) also use 1:12 at the steepest, 1:15 and 1:20 for higher level differences. |
| 2a | MoHUA Harmonised Guidelines 2021: max ramp gradient 1:12 | https://www.mohua.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/03/8db1bdd5f890ef8949337f084e4596d0.pdf (HG 2021, March 2022 print). Kerb ramp: cl. 3.7.2 Gradient. External ramps: cl. 3.10.2 with Table 3.4 | 3.7.2: "The gradient of a kerb ramp should not be steeper than 1:12; the flared sides should not be more than 1:10." Table 3.4: "GRADIENT OF RAMP 1:12 1:14 1:15 1:20 ... Maximum length of horizontal run-in metres 6 9 11 15 18" | PARTLY | 1:12 is the steepest permitted for kerb ramps (3.7.2) and for external ramps (Table 3.4, only up to a 6 m run). Longer runs need gentler slopes (1:14, 1:15, 1:20; "NOT GENTLER THAN 1:25" is the last column). So "max 1:12" is right as a ceiling, not as the single value. Curved ramps should be 1:16 or gentler (3.10.2). |
| 2b | HG 2021: minimum clear footpath width 1200 mm | same PDF, cl. 3.5.1 Widths and Heights (Access Routes / Walkways, p. 75) | "Minimum walkway widths shall be 1200 mm. However, for two way traffic they should be 1800mm wide. In exceptional cases (such as around trees/poles etc.); the recommended clear width could be 1500mm." | PARTLY | 1200 mm is the minimum for a walkway/access route, and the text says "walkway", not "footpath". Two-way is 1800 mm. The kerb ramp figures show "Pathway min width 1800". Other HG 2021 copies number this cl. 3.4.1 (e.g. https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s35d6646aad9bcc0be55b2c82f69750387/uploads/2021/11/2021110234.pdf, Sept 2021 version), so cite the edition. Ramp minimum clear width is also 1200 mm (cl. 3.10.3). Kerb ramp width is 900 mm minimum (cl. 3.7.3). |
| 3 | IRC:103-2012 cl. 6.2.1: max pavement height 150 mm | https://law.resource.org/pub/in/bis/irc/irc.gov.in.103.2012.pdf, cl. 6.2.1 Kerb height | "Maximum height of a pavement (including kerb, walking surface, top-of-paving) shall not exceed 150 mm from the road level, which is the standard anthropometric height of a public step/ riser." | CONFIRMED | Exact match to the claim, the claim's quote ends before ", which is the standard...".
