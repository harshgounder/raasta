| **AI-04** | Evaluate answers to an Indian university's published policy or circulars. | **Proposed scenario.** Use the actual document as ground truth; do not fabricate a regulation or college policy. |
| **W3-01** | Anonymous scholarship or campus-benefit eligibility. | **Proposed scenario**, not Aadhaar integration. The DPDP timetable supports careful privacy framing, not a claim that ZK alone establishes legal compliance. [43] |
| **W3-02** | A student gives an agent a tightly bounded purchasing allowance. | **Proposed scenario using test tokens.** No RBI authorization, bank integration, or UPI equivalence is established for the prototype. |
| **CL-01** | Review a selected Indian forest-edge or vegetation-change patch. | **Proposed local application.** No India-wide forest-loss statistic is asserted here; the base labels NDVI-drop results as potential deforestation. [24] |
| **CL-02** | Explain estimated upstream emissions in a municipal or campus procurement table. | **Proposed scenario.** Factor geography, units, price year, and Scope 3 boundaries must be visible; no universal Indian Scope 3 mandate is asserted. [33] |
| **CL-03** | Hostel cooling demand during a hot-weather scenario. | **Verified policy context:** India's Cooling Action Plan targets reduced cooling demand and energy requirements by **2037-38**. These are goals, not measured prototype savings. [38] |
| **SC-01** | Investigate a vehicle's candidate path across consented campus-camera clips. | **Proposed scenario.** Use authorized footage; DPDP notification is legal context, not a surveillance-compliance certificate. [43] |
| **SC-02** | A photo-backed route between a few MUJ campus destinations. | **Proposed local pilot.** It is not a verified campus accessibility audit or a statutory-compliance determination. |
| **HM-01** | Reproducible chemistry analysis connected to the importance of pharmaceutical standards. | **Verified:** the Indian Pharmacopoeia specifies standards concerning identity, purity, and strength. Computed descriptors are not equivalent to those quality tests. [40] |
| **HM-02** | Indian brand-to-composition confirmation before duplicate/interaction review. | **Proposed bounded checker.** Use checked references and pharmacist review; no CDSCO approval or comprehensive Indian clinical coverage is established. The Indian Pharmacopoeia/National Formulary context should not be presented as endorsement. [40] |

**Takeaway:** The strongest Indian pitch uses a real local problem and a modest, auditable claim. Avoid attaching an impressive national statistic to a capability it does not measure.

## The Overnight Build: One Backbone, One Story, One Hostile Test

### The recommended AI-03 case study

Build around a fictional student, **Mira**, who believes three suspicious messages are unrelated. The opening screen contains the messages, not a feature list. Within ten seconds, a shared identifier appears, followed by the phrase **"Three stories. One connection worth checking."** That wording is intentionally narrower than "one criminal network."

The substantive implementation is a source-backed graph with topic augmentation. A judge clicks an edge and sees a quotation; edits an identifier and sees a link retract; uploads a new document and sees the real backend update the graph. A topic label such as "advance-fee request" must be identified as an inferred classification unless a source explicitly states it.

The cinematic layer should serve this evidence trail. Use restrained camera movement to focus on the relevant path, then stop the animation when a source opens. A static or 2D fallback should preserve the entire workflow if WebGL is slow. Sound begins only after a user action; captions, mute, reduced motion, and a persistent **Skip to live demo** control keep the presentation usable.

### Proposed 12-hour allocation

| Time | Deliverable | Stop/go condition |
|---|---|---|
| **0-1 hour** | Confirm full statement, reuse rules, licenses, credentials; launch the chosen backbone. | If the backend cannot run, cut scope immediately. |
| **1-3 hours** | Create synthetic fixtures, source identifiers, topic/entity schema, and one API contract. | Every displayed relationship has a provenance field. |
| **3-6 hours** | Complete ingestion -> extraction -> topic augmentation -> graph -> evidence panel. | A new input changes the result through the actual backend. |
| **6-8 hours** | Add story, graph transitions, source focus, and mobile-readable layout. | The story works without sound and without 3D effects. |
| **8-10 hours** | Test wrong identifiers, contradictions, malicious document instructions, timeouts, and missing data. | At least one uncertainty or rejection path is demonstrated. |
| **10-11 hours** | Deploy from a clean build; verify environment variables and backup assets. | A teammate can open the public URL and complete the workflow. |
| **11-12 hours** | Rehearse, record a clearly labeled backup, freeze features. | No dependency upgrade or major rewrite. |

**Takeaway:** Spend the middle of the night on the evidence-bearing vertical slice. Presentation polish comes after the application can survive an unfamiliar input.

### A three-minute judging script - proposed

1. **0:00-0:15 - Human stakes:** "Mira thought these messages were unrelated."
2. **0:15-0:30 - Reveal:** Show the connection and a plain-language explanation.
3. **0:30-1:10 - Proof:** Click two edges, show their quotations, and ingest one new document.
4. **1:10-1:50 - What-if:** Change an identifier or introduce conflicting evidence. Let the application retract or qualify its conclusion.
5. **1:50-2:30 - Contribution:** Explain what the reused backbone provides and what you added: topics, provenance, conflict handling, and the interface.
6. **2:30-3:00 - Close:** State the bounded use case, limitations, and next validation step.

AWS's approximately three-minute video requirement is a useful presentation precedent, not a confirmed Hypnexis time limit. [7]

### The checks that make the show defensible

| Judge's challenge | What the application should show |
|---|---|
| "Is this hardcoded?" | A previously unseen, bounded input changing the output; fixtures labeled as fixtures. |
| "What if the AI is wrong?" | Evidence, uncertainty, and a correction/retraction path. |
| "What if the internet fails?" | An honestly labeled cached example or recording, not a simulated live success. |
| "What did your team build?" | A clear boundary between reused components and your contribution. |
| "What if the document tells the model to ignore instructions?" | Document text treated as untrusted input; the attempted instruction does not become application authority. |
| "Where did this statistic or relationship come from?" | A source record or exact quotation, not another generated explanation. |
| "Is the data private?" | Synthetic/authorized inputs and a truthful explanation of external processing. |

**Takeaway:** Do not precompute a fake success and call it live. A modest real result plus an honest fallback is more defensible than a spectacular interface whose central action cannot be inspected.

## Synthesis

The major options differ in **mechanism, dependency burden, evidence, and consequence of error**. Those differences matter more than how futuristic their landing pages look.

| Strategy family | Main mechanism | Overnight dependency | Best evidence in the demo | Principal trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AI-03 / AI-04** | Structured evidence and diagnostic decomposition | Model setup, small corpus, references | Quotations, controlled failures, retractable links | Easy to visualize; vulnerable to misleading associations or evaluator overconfidence. |
| **AI-01 / SC-01** | Learned signal or appearance classification | Checkpoints, representative inputs, inference | Controls, difficult examples, candidate uncertainty | Emotionally immediate; domain-shift risk is difficult to resolve overnight. |
| **W3-01 / W3-02** | Cryptographic proof or contract enforcement | Wallet/chain tooling and precise trust assumptions | Invalid proof or forbidden action actually failing | Strong falsifiability; setup and semantics can consume the night. |
| **CL-01 / CL-02 / CL-03** | Observation, estimation, or forecasting | Appropriate imagery, factors, or time series | Dates, units, baselines, uncertainty | Visually compelling; a beautiful output can conceal unsuitable data. |
| **SC-02 / HM-02** | Decision support in a consequential human workflow | Reviewed local data or medical references | Traceable barrier/ingredient and an abstention path | High empathy; "safe" is a much stronger claim than the prototype can establish. |
| **AI-02 / HM-01** | Constrained analysis and reproducible computation | A qualifying local model or chemistry environment | Reconciled queries or versioned analysis artifacts | Easier to audit; must avoid becoming a generic dashboard or decorative viewer. |

**Takeaway:** The common denominator is not "more AI." It is a transformation the judge can understand, followed by evidence the judge can challenge.

Three tensions should guide the final decision. First, **the most emotional claim can be the hardest to validate**: voice-clone detection and prescription checking carry more immediate stakes than a knowledge graph, but that also makes false reassurance more consequential. Second, **the most rigorous mechanism can be the least familiar**: ZK and wallet policies need an everyday story, yet their actual verification/rejection can be exceptionally convincing. Third, **the most reusable repository may not contain the hardest missing asset**: an accessibility map still needs local barrier data, and a carbon calculator still needs appropriate factors. The underlying sources make those dependency distinctions visible. [9][20][15][18][16][33]

The winning-project evidence supports both sides of the balance. Vite Vere and Outdraw AI demonstrate legible human interactions; RiskWise and PRism make technical analysis consumable; AWS's rubric makes clear that execution still matters heavily. These examples justify a presentation strategy, not a prediction of the Hypnexis result. [3][4][25][7]

**Final recommendation: choose AI-03 and build "One Scam. Three Stories."** Fork LightRAG as the backbone, implement explicit topic augmentation and source provenance, and make a correction/retraction the second big demo moment. If your opening astonishes the judge and your next click answers "How do you know?", the cinematic layer is doing useful work rather than hiding a weak system.
