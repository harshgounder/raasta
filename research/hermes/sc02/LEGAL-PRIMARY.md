## 1. S. Rajaseekaran v. Union of India & Ors., Supreme Court of India

- **Document:** Order in I.A. No. 50798/2025 (and two connected IAs), Record of Proceedings
- **Court:** Supreme Court of India, Writ Petition (Civil) No. 295/2012
- **Bench as printed:** Hon'ble Mr. Justice Abhay S. Oka, Hon'ble Mr. Justice Ujjal Bhuyan
- **Date as printed:** 14-05-2025
- **Citation as printed:** none. The document carries no neutral citation; it is a "RECORD OF PROCEEDINGS" with "WRIT PETITION (CIVIL) NO(S).295/2012". Cite it as *S. Rajaseekaran v. Union of India*, W.P. (C) No. 295/2012, order dated 14 May 2025.
- **URL:** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/166094161/
- **Local file:** `raw/legal/ik_166094161.txt` (from `ik_166094161.html`)
- **fetched_at:** 2026-10-24T17:14+05:30
- **Status:** PRIMARY TEXT READ. Source is Indian Kanoon's reproduction of the Supreme Court Record of Proceedings (the SCI PDF for this particular order was not located). Text is complete: cause title, coram, order paragraphs, signed by Asst. Registrar-cum-PS and Court Master.

Verbatim quotes:

> "The right to have footpaths or footways which are in good condition is certainly an essential part of Article 21 of the Constitution of India."

> "by the side of all public roads, there must be proper facility of footpaths or footways with special emphasis on user-friendly to persons with disabilities."

> "We, therefore, direct all the States and Union Territories to come out with guidelines in terms of the directions issued by the High Courts as mentioned above."

Why this is the anchor document: it is the only Supreme Court text read in this exercise that joins three things in one paragraph, Article 21, footpaths on every public road, and usability by persons with disabilities. It also records that the applicant relied on Indian Roads Congress standards, and it directs every State and UT to frame guidelines and file compliance reports within two months. List date printed: 1 August 2025.

Named inside this order (with citations as printed, but NOT read by me):
- *High Court on its own Motion v. State of Maharashtra & Ors.*, 2018 SCC OnLine Bom 21221
- *D.S. Ramachandra Reddy v. The Commissioner of Police, Bangalore & Ors.*, 2021 SCC OnLine Kar 12223

## 2. Maniyar Iliyaz @ Shaik Riyaz & Anr. v. P. Ayyappan & Ors., Supreme Court of India

- **Document:** Judgment, 13 pages, Reportable
- **Court:** Supreme Court of India, Civil Appellate Jurisdiction
- **Case number as printed:** Civil Appeal No(s). 4665-4666/2025
- **Citation as printed:** 2026 INSC 647
- **Bench as printed:** Pamidighantam Sri Narasimha, Atul S. Chandurkar
- **Date as printed:** June 19, 2026 (New Delhi)
- **URL:** https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2024/42514/42514_2024_3_1501_71888_Judgement_19-Jun-2026.pdf
- **Local files:** `raw/legal/SC_INRE_FundamentalRightToWalk_2026-06-19.pdf`, `raw/legal/SC_walk_2026-06-19.txt`
- **fetched_at:** 2026-10-24T16:56+05:30
- **Status:** PRIMARY TEXT READ (court's own PDF, text layer intact).

Verbatim quotes:

> "The citizen’s fundamental right to walk on a demarcated footpath is primary and shall have priority over movement by motorised vehicles."

> "If a road exists, there must then be a duty to ensure that a footpath is demarcated and maintained for the walkers. This is an enforceable duty."

> "The fundamental right to walk will take within its sweep the right to demarcated footpaths."

Context that must travel with these quotes: this is a motor-accident compensation appeal (a five-year-old killed while walking to school, "there was neither a footpath nor a pedestrian crossing"). The Court used it to declare the right to walk, list the duty bearers (urban development authorities, municipal corporations, municipalities, panchayats), hold that violation gives a restitutionary remedy independent of the Motor Vehicles Act, and direct the Registry to send the judgment to the Ministries of Housing and Urban Affairs, Rural Development, and Road Transport and Highways, and to the Law Commission. It awarded Rs. 11,44,628 in compensation.

Cause-title point: para 21 directs the Registry to "re-number this case as a petition under Article 32 of the Constitution by changing the cause title to Re: Fundamental Right to Walk and Footpath". So the re-titled name appears in the judgment but the citation and cause title printed at the head of the document are Maniyar Iliyaz v. P. Ayyappan.

Critical limit: this judgment does **not** mention disability, accessibility standards, ramps, kerbs, or IRC at all. It is the right-to-walk case, not the disability-access case.

## 3. Rajive Raturi v. Union of India & Ors., Supreme Court of India, 2024

- **Document:** Judgment, 51 pages, Reportable, signed 2024.11.08 15:51:08 IST
- **Court:** Supreme Court of India, Civil Original Jurisdiction
- **Case number as printed:** Writ Petition (C) No. 243 of 2005, with Writ Petition (C) No. 228 of 2006
- **Citation as printed:** 2024 INSC 858
- **Bench as printed:** Dr Dhananjaya Y Chandrachud, CJI; J. B. Pardiwala; Manoj Misra
- **Date as printed:** November 08, 2024
- **URL:** https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2005/9321/9321_2005_1_1503_56986_Judgement_08-Nov-2024.pdf
- **Local files:** `raw/legal/SC_RajiveRaturi_2024-11-08_2024INSC858.pdf`, `raw/legal/SC_2024-11-08.txt`
- **fetched_at:** 2026-10-24T16:47+05:30
- **Status:** PRIMARY TEXT READ (court's own PDF).

Verbatim quotes:

> "A ceiling without a floor is hardly a sturdy structure."

> "Creating a minimum floor of accessibility cannot be left to the altar of “progressive realization”."

> "The Union Government is, accordingly, directed to delineate mandatory rules, as required by Section 40, within a period of three months from the date of this Judgment."

What it actually decides: Rule 15(1) of the RPWD Rules is ultra vires the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 because the guidelines it prescribes are recommendatory rather than mandatory. The Union Government must separate non-negotiable rules from guidelines within three months, in consultation with stakeholders, with NALSAR-CDS involved. States and UTs must apply the consequences in Sections 44, 45, 46 and 89, "including the holding back of completion certificates and imposition of fines". The writ petitions were adjourned to 7 March 2025 for a compliance report.

Footpath content in this judgment: exactly one sentence, and it is the Court reporting the NALSAR-CDS findings, not a direction:

> "In contrast, inaccessible infrastructure, such as pothole-filled footpaths, hinders mobility and safety."

That sentence sits inside the summary of the NALSAR report at para 53(e). It is quotable as a finding recorded by the Court, not as an operative direction.

## 4. Rajive Raturi v. Union of India, Supreme Court of India, 2017 (the eleven action points)

- **Document:** Judgment, Reportable, 87 pages in the reproduction
- **Court:** Supreme Court of India, Civil Original Jurisdiction
- **Case number as printed:** Writ Petition (Civil) No. 243 of 2005, with Writ Petition (Civil) No. 228 of 2006
- **Author as printed:** A.K. Sikri, J. The closing line states the judgment was "pronounced ... of the Bench comprising His Lordship and Hon'ble Mr. Justice Ashok Bhushan."
- **Date as printed:** 15 December 2017 (digital signature 2017.12.15 14:20:08 IST)
- **Equivalent citation as printed by the reporter:** AIRONLINE 2018 SC 544
- **URL:** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/149818296/
- **Local files:** `raw/legal/IK_RajiveRaturi_2017-12-15.html`, `raw/legal/IK_2017-12-15.txt`
- **fetched_at:** 2026-10-24T16:44+05:30
- **Status:** PRIMARY TEXT READ (Indian Kanoon reproduction of the judgment; the Supreme Court's own PDF was not located).

Verbatim quotes:

> "we direct these Advisory Boards to be constituted by all States and Union Territories within a period of three months from today"

> "Providing for unobstructed footpaths with minimum hindrances in such manner so as to leave obstacle-free walking areas in a straight line on the footpath"

> "it becomes the duty of the Union, States as well as Union Territories to ensure that all Government buses are disabled friendly"

Attribution warning on the second quote: that sentence is in the petitioner's pleaded list of measures for roads, recorded by the Court at the start of the judgment ("As per the petitioner ... would imply the following features"). It is **not** the Court's own direction. It can be used only with the words "the petitioner's pleaded measures, recorded in the judgment" attached.

What the operative part does contain: eleven numbered directions, all about buildings, airports, railways, buses, websites, National Building Code, sign-language interpreters, and State Advisory Boards. Pedestrian infrastructure is not among the eleven heads. The petitioner's comment table does record, about the Accessible India campaign, that "Pedestrian infrastructure not included as targets in AI campaign", and notes that sidewalks, footpaths, kerb ramps and pedestrian crossings sit in Section 11 of the Harmonized Guidelines. Again: petitioner's submission, not holding.

Bench discrepancy to keep off any slide: the 2024 judgment describes the 2017 bench as "Justice AK Sikri and Justice Abdul Nazeer", while the 2017 judgment itself names Ashok Bhushan. Do not name the 2017 bench.

## 5. High Court of Judicature at Bombay v. Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai & Ors., Bombay High Court

- **Court:** High Court of Judicature at Bombay, Ordinary Original Civil Jurisdiction
- **Case number as printed:** SUO MOTU PUBLIC INTEREST LITIGATION NO. 3 OF 2023
- **Bench as printed:** Devendra Kumar Upadhyaya, CJ. and Arif S. Doctor, J.
- **Date as printed:** 13th MARCH 2024
- **Citation as printed:** none
- **URL:** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/189115029/
- **Local files:** `raw/legal/ik_189115029.html`, `raw/legal/ik_189115029.txt`
- **fetched_at:** 2026-10-24T17:14+05:30
- **Status:** PRIMARY TEXT READ (Indian Kanoon reproduction; the Bombay HC PDF was not fetched).

Verbatim quotes:

> "which shall contain the details of the steps taken by the Board for providing easy access to persons with disabilities to footpaths in the city of Mumbai"

> "removal of all the discrepancies concerning the bollards on the footpaths to provide better accessibility to differently abled persons and old persons"

Attribution warning on the second quote: it is the Court recording a statement by senior counsel for MCGM, not a finding. First quote is the Court's own direction: the Government Pleader must file an affidavit sworn by a high-level officer of the State Advisory Board constituted under Section 71 of the RPWD Act, 2016.

Why it is useful for a pitch: a High Court, in a suo motu PIL, treating "easy access to persons with disabilities to footpaths in the city of Mumbai" as something the State Advisory Board must account for on affidavit. Bollards, not absent footpaths, were the immediate subject.

## 6. IRC:103-2012, Guidelines for Pedestrian Facilities (First Revision)

- **Document:** Indian Roads Congress code of practice, "GUIDELINES FOR PEDESTRIAN FACILITIES (First Revision)", published May 2012, price Rs. 600. First published February 1989; reprinted July 2004 and August 2007; first revision May 2012.
- **Institution:** Indian Roads Congress, Kama Koti Marg, Sector-6, R.K. Puram, New Delhi-110 022
- **Date as printed:** MAY, 2012
- **Citation as printed:** IRC:103-2012
- **URL:** https://law.resource.org/pub/in/bis/irc/irc.gov.in.103.2012.pdf
- **Local files:** `raw/legal/IRC103-2012_law.resource.org.pdf` (2.9 MB), `raw/legal/IRC103-2012.txt`
- **fetched_at:** 2026-10-24T16:53+05:30
- **Status:** PRIMARY TEXT READ (full published code, text layer intact).

Verbatim quotes, clause 6.2.1 "Kerb height":

> "Maximum height of a pavement (including kerb, walking surface, top-of-paving) shall not exceed 150 mm from the road level"

Verbatim quotes, clause 6.1.5.1 "Width":

> "The minimum width of a clear unobstructed pathway should be 1800 mm in roads of right of way of 10 m and above."

> "This allows two wheelchairs to pass each other comfortably."

Two further clauses worth having, paraphrase only unless re-quoted from the file: clause 6.1.4 requires a clear height of 2.2 m across the walking zone, and clause 6.2.3 requires kerb ramp width "not less than 1200 mm" with a tactile warning strip. Clause 6.1.5.1 also allows 1500 mm where right of way is under 10 m, and a 1200 mm reduced width for short distances where trees or walls constrain the path.

Status of this document: it is a code of practice published by a professional body, not a statute. The 14 May 2025 Supreme Court order records that the applicant relied on IRC standards but does not adopt them as a mandate.

# What the deck may and may not claim

## Safe claims (each traces to a document above)

1. The Supreme Court has held that footpaths in good condition are part of Article 21. Safe, from the 14 May 2025 order in S. Rajaseekaran, W.P. (C) No. 295/2012. Quote the sentence exactly.
2. The Supreme Court has directed every State and Union Territory to frame footpath guidelines and file compliance reports, and recorded that footpaths must be usable by persons with disabilities. Safe, same order, same date. Say "guidelines in terms of the directions issued by the High Courts"; do not say "the Court adopted IRC:103".
3. The Supreme Court has declared a fundamental right to walk on demarcated footpaths, priority over motorised vehicles, with municipal bodies as duty bearers and a restitutionary remedy for violation. Safe, 2026 INSC 647, Civil Appeal Nos. 4665-4666/2025, 19 June 2026.
4. "If a road exists, there must then be a duty to ensure that a footpath is demarcated and maintained." Safe, same judgment, para 10.
5. The Supreme Court struck down Rule 15(1) of the RPWD Rules as ultra vires and gave the Union three months to prescribe mandatory accessibility rules, with completion certificates and fines as consequences. Safe, 2024 INSC 858, 8 November 2024.
6. A High Court has ordered a State Advisory Board to file an affidavit on the steps taken to give persons with disabilities easy access to Mumbai's footpaths. Safe, Bombay High Court, Suo Motu PIL No. 3 of 2023, 13 March 2024.
7. Indian design standards put the kerb height cap at 150 mm and the minimum clear footpath width at 1800 mm on roads of 10 m right of way or more. Safe as a statement about IRC:103-2012, clauses 6.2.1 and 6.1.5.1. Always say "IRC:103-2012 says", never "the law requires".

## Forbidden claims

1. Do not say the Supreme Court ordered footpaths to IRC:103 dimensions. No document read says that. The 14 May 2025 order only notes that the applicant relied on IRC standards.
2. Do not present 1800 mm or 150 mm as statutory minimums. IRC is a code of practice. No statute read incorporates it.
3. Do not describe 2026 INSC 647 as a disability or accessibility judgment. It never uses the words disability, accessible, ramp, kerb or IRC. Its holding is the right to walk.
4. Do not describe Rajive Raturi as a footpath case. Its subject is Rule 15 and mandatory accessibility standards; footpaths appear once, as a NALSAR finding.
5. Do not quote the 2017 "unobstructed footpaths" passage as the Court's holding. It is the petitioner's pleaded measure. If used at all, label it as such.
6. Do not name the 2017 bench. The 2017 judgment and the 2024 judgment disagree (Ashok Bhushan vs Abdul Nazeer).
7. Do not print a case number, neutral citation, or date that is not printed on the document. Specifically: the 14 May 2025 order has no neutral citation on its face, so cite it by writ petition number and date only. Do not attach "2025 INSC 1189" to it without seeing that citation on a document, it comes from a secondary tracker, not from the text I read.
8. Do not use the cause title "Re: Fundamental Right to Walk and Footpath" as if it were the formal citation. It is the re-numbered cause title the Court directed in para 21; the printed citation remains 2026 INSC 647, Maniyar Iliyaz v. P. Ayyappan.
9. Do not cite any CAG finding. Nothing from CAG was read. Do not use the "Footpath developed crack and was tilted" line.
10. Do not state later procedural history of Rajive Raturi (extensions in 2026, listing dates after 7 March 2025). Only "adjourned to 7 March 2025" is on the 2024 judgment. Anything later came from a secondary tracker and is unverified.
