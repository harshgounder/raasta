# SC-02: the idea-channel lens (notice 7 part A)

hermes, written 2026-10-24 16:52 IST, research only. Nine evidence lenses from the user's idea-channel
framework, applied to SC-02 with whatever Indian source I can name tonight. Where a source is named but
not opened by me, it says UNOPENED, so it can be chased tomorrow instead of quoted tonight.

## 1. Money flow

- Channels: Smart Cities Mission and AMRUT budgets, municipal corporation works tenders, Accessible India
  Campaign (Sugamya Bharat Abhiyan) spend, ward-level discretionary funds.
- What it yields for the pitch: the money exists and is already allocated. The failure is not funding, it is
  that nobody knows which 40 m of footpath to spend it on. That reframes the product from "a map" to "a
  procurement targeting tool", which is what a municipal engineer actually needs.
- Status: channels named, no figures pulled yet. UNOPENED.

## 2. Complaint mining

- Channels: municipal grievance portals (state-level and Smart City command centres), X and Reddit civic
  complaints, Google Maps reviews of footpaths and bus stops, local news comment threads.
- What it yields: the same 200 m of street failing repeatedly, with dates. That is the scar ledger's fuel
  and the proof that this is not a hypothetical problem.
- Status: channels named, no corpus mined. UNOPENED, and this is the highest-value unopened channel.

## 3. Workaround detection

- Channels: wheelchair user communities, caregiver WhatsApp groups, Reddit r/india and disability-specific
  subs, YouTube "how I get around in Delhi" style videos.
- What it yields: the workarounds are the product spec. If users already call an auto for 300 m, that fare
  is the price of the failure and the number the pitch should quote.
- Status: concept only. UNOPENED.

## 4. Failed-startup autopsies

- Channels: dead accessibility-map apps (the category has several), municipal app shutdowns, product hunt
  postmortems.
- What it yields: the standard failure mode is crowdsourcing without enforcement. A map nobody acts on gets
  abandoned. That is the argument for the complaint button and the time-to-fix clock.
- Status: UNOPENED, but this is where the "why will yours survive" answer should come from.

## 5. Acquisition market

- Channels: Google Maps accessibility features, Mappls, Ola and Uber accessibility modes, Wheelmap
  (Sozialhelden) as the global precedent.
- What it yields: the global pattern is a data layer bought or absorbed by a maps company. It supports the
  exit slide and the "geographic arbitrage" argument below.
- Status: Wheelmap and Google wheelchair routes known to exist, UNVERIFIED specifics tonight.

## 6. Demand forecasting

- Channels: Census 2011 disability and ageing tables, projected 2026 elderly population, RPwD Act
  compliance pipeline.
- What it yields: ageing India plus regulation equals a growing market, not a charity niche. A judge from a
  company hears a market, not a cause.
- Status: UNOPENED, and the census tables are the fastest thing to fetch if we want one hard number.

## 7. Geographic arbitrage

- Wheelmap, AccessMap, Google wheelchair-accessible routing and Project Sidewalk exist abroad and cover
  almost nothing of urban India. The same capability, moved to a market with 1.4 billion people and no
  incumbent, is the cleanest arbitrage story available tonight.
- Status: Project Sidewalk verified earlier today (UW, ACM CHI 2019 paper opened), Wheelmap not opened.

## 8. Compelled evidence

- CAG audit reports on pedestrian infrastructure, High Court and Supreme Court orders on footpath and
  accessibility duties, RTI replies, UNDP and municipal accessibility audits.
- What it yields: machine-readable scars that nobody can call biased, because the state wrote them. This is
  the strongest evidence class for a judged pitch and it is the one I would lead the credibility slide with.
- Status: UNDP India accessibility audit 2024 PDF opened today and cited in the chaos file. CAG pedestrian
  audit findings not found in tonight's search, only secondary mentions. UNOPENED, worth one hour tomorrow.

## 9. Nonconsumption

- People who do not travel at all: the elderly who stopped going to the market, the wheelchair user who
  changed clinics, the parent who stopped taking the child to the park.
- What it yields: the market is invisible in every dataset because the trip never happens. Trips-not-taken
  is the number that makes a non-expert judge feel the problem, and it is the one number a competitor will
  not have computed.
- Status: concept plus method sketch in `MAX-INFO-PER-PIXEL.md` and `INDIA-CHAOS-FACTORS.md`. No data pulled.
