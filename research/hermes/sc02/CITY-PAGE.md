# /city, Jaipur

## Block 1
**Six million people, one unequal footpath**
Jaipur district holds 6,626,178 people, and 3,471,847 of them live in the urban part where a footpath
should exist. The number matters because every route we price is a share of those people, not an abstraction.
Source: Census of India 2011, Table C-13 rural and urban split, catalog 1465, fetched 2026-10-24.

## Block 2
**One in eleven needs a wider path**
455,130 residents are 60 or older and 158,909 live with a disability, which together is 575,503 people or
8.69 percent of the district. That is the share of this city for whom a 150 mm kerb is not a detail.
Source: Census 2011 Tables C-13 and C-20. The 575,503 is our arithmetic, the two counts are the census.

## Block 3
**Thirty thousand with a mobility disability**
30,874 people in Jaipur district report a disability in movement, the exact group a footpath either serves
or excludes. A route that works for them works for a pram, a trolley and a delivery cart too.
Source: Census 2011, Table C-20, Jaipur district rows, fetched 2026-10-24.

## Block 4
**Coaching hub footpaths blocked by cafes**
Outside Jaipur's coaching classes, students say the footpath is taken by cafe furniture and stalls most days.
"I try to use the footpath every day, but it is usually blocked," says Riya Sharma, a student.
Source: Times of India Jaipur, 4 August 2026, jaipurs-footpaths-remain-blocked-despite-sc-push.

## Block 5
**C-Scheme offices park on the pavement**
In C-Scheme and Ashok Nagar, office vehicles are routinely parked across the footpath rather than beside it.
Residents say enforcement appears for a week and then stops, and the pavement fills again.
Source: Times of India Jaipur, 4 August 2026, same article, plus 22 June 2026 MI Road report.

## Block 6
**Vegetable market takes pavement and road**
At Sahakar Marg an unauthorised vegetable market occupies the pavement and part of the carriageway at once.
Pedestrians are pushed into moving traffic in the same metres where a footpath exists on paper.
Source: Times of India Jaipur, 4 August 2026, jaipurs-footpaths-remain-blocked-despite-sc-push.

## Block 7

Shopkeepers on MI Road say illegal parking on the footpath costs them customers, not just pedestrians.

## Block 8

Sellers cleared from one spot reappear on the next footpath, because the clearance had no place to go.
"We used to sit near Mansarovar Metro Station. After we were removed from there, we shifted here," says Ramesh.

## Block 9

Near the RTO office in Jhalana, a whole stretch of footpath has been occupied for more than two decades.
"We were brought here for building work back in 2001. Since then, we've stayed here," says Rama Devi.
Source: Times of India Jaipur, 21 August 2025, same article.

Around Ram Mandir Cinema, footpaths have effectively disappeared under structures that are no longer temporary.

Source: Times of India Jaipur, 21 August 2025, same article.

## Block 12
**JDA's own walkway fails its own rule**
Outside JDA headquarters on JLN Marg, an official says more than 90 percent of the walking space is landscaping.
The unobstructed strip left is so narrow that two people can barely walk side by side.
Source: Times of India Jaipur, 26 July 2026, jda-hq-under-lens-for-pavement-encroachment.

## Block 13
**The Supreme Court called walking a right**
In June 2026 the Supreme Court held that walking on a demarcated footpath is a fundamental right, and that it
takes priority over motorised movement. A blocked footpath is therefore a rights failure, not a design taste.
Source: 2026 INSC 647, 19 June 2026, quoted in research/hermes/sc02/LEGAL-PRIMARY.md.

## Block 14
**A ceiling without a floor**
The Court also wrote that a ceiling without a floor is hardly a sturdy structure, rejecting standards that
look generous on paper while nothing is built underneath. We quote it because it describes every unfunded norm.
Source: 2024 INSC 858, Rajive Raturi v Union of India, quoted in research/hermes/sc02/LEGAL-PRIMARY.md.

# /how, standards in plain words

## Block 15
**Two codes, three degrees apart**
The 2012 Indian Roads Congress code allowed a kerb ramp as steep as 1:12, the 2022 revision asks for 1:15,
which is gentler. MoHUA's 2021 accessibility guidelines still print 1:12 as the ceiling, so the codes disagree.
Source: IRC:103-2012 cl 6.2.3; IRC:103-2022 cl 6.7 and Fig. 48; MoHUA HG 2021 cl 3.7.2. Verified in
research/claude/VERIFY-STANDARDS.md.

## Block 16
**So which slope do we judge against**
We judge against the gentler of the applicable numbers, 1:15, and we print which code and year we used.
A wheelchair user does not care which committee wrote the steeper allowance.
Source: as Block 15, and the rule is stated on every receipt.

## Block 17
**The kerb may rise 150 mm**
The national code caps footpath height at 150 mm above the road, the height of a standard public step.
Above that, without a ramp, a wheelchair cannot mount it at all, which is why we print centimetres, not adjectives.
Source: IRC:103-2012 cl 6.2.1, verbatim: "Maximum height of a pavement (including kerb, walking surface,
top-of-paving) shall not exceed 150 mm from the road level".

## Block 18
**A walkway starts at 1200 mm**
MoHUA sets 1200 mm as the minimum clear walkway, and 1800 mm where people pass in both directions.
We measure clear width, not total width, because a pole and a stall are what actually shrink the path.
Source: MoHUA HG 2021 cl 3.5.1 Widths and Heights.

## Block 19
**We print the number and its date**
Every measurement on this site carries the photograph it came from and the day it was taken.
If the photo is three years old, the answer says three years old, because a footpath can be fixed after the picture.
Source: our own method, shown on every receipt.

## Block 20
**When we do not know, we say so**
A path we have not seen is marked unknown, not safe, and it still adds an uncertainty cost to your route.
In the demo city, 42 of 42 segments were unknown at the 19:50 snapshot, and the site says that out loud.
Source: app/site-data/jaipur/routes.json (segments 42, unknown 42), verified in
research/hermes/sc02/FACTCHECK-SITE.md. RE-CHECK THIS BLOCK before publishing: the routing lane is attaching
real GPS detections, and the honest number is whatever the file says at build time, not this snapshot.
