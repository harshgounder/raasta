## Notes, gaps, dead ends

- **The split is the story.** Three independent sources make the same argument with different methods: RAGChecker (claim-level retriever versus generator metrics), ARES (separate judges per stage plus confidence intervals), and the Japanese and Russian write-ups (do not trust one score). A demo that shows stage-level blame is the differentiated angle.
- **RAGAS repo is the least recently touched of the big five** (last commit 2026-02-24 against 2026-10-23/24 for DeepEval, TruLens, Opik, Langfuse). Worth checking if the project moved or slowed before betting on it.
- **Licence traps**: Phoenix is non-OSI (GitHub reports Other), Langfuse is MIT core with a commercial `ee` directory, RAGBench and the USTC CRAG repo have no licence file at all. Do not vendor those into a submission without reading the terms.
- **RAGBench and the CRAG competition repos are stale** (2024). They are still the best public exam sets, but they will not reflect 2026 model behaviour.
- **Blocked pages**: CSDN (security wall), Juejin (network block), Devpost judge gallery (bot block). No facts were taken from them beyond search snippets, and the one Devpost row is marked UNVERIFIED.
- **Missing category**: no open-source tool found that ships a signed, reproducible "eval report" artifact a judge can verify offline. Everything either runs in a hosted dashboard or prints numbers to a terminal. That is a gap a hackathon entry could fill.
