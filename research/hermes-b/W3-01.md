### The Acceptance Contract For W3-01

Use the following as the acceptance specification for the assembled product. These are engineering requirements proposed for this problem, not claims that every candidate already satisfies them.

| Boundary | Required behavior | Visible test |
|---|---|---|
| Trusted issuer | The accepted issuer key is fixed by policy or an authenticated registry, not freely chosen by the holder | A credential from a second issuer is rejected |
| Signed field binding | The exact private field used in the predicate is covered by the verified signature | Changing age or income breaks proof generation or verification |
| Signature constraint | Successful signature verification is enforced by circuit constraints | A malformed signature fails |
| Predicate constraint | The comparison or equality is enforced inside the proof | An ineligible signed credential fails |
| Public policy | The relevant threshold, program, or verifier context cannot be silently replaced | A proof for another program is rejected |
| Contract connection | The contract checks the proof and relevant public inputs before granting access | A direct call without a valid proof fails |
| Privacy boundary | No raw credential field is accidentally included in calldata, logs, analytics, or error reports | The receipt inspector shows only the intended public inputs |
| Replay boundary | If one redemption is promised, the application enforces it | The second redemption fails without disclosing identity |

**Decision:** A hidden number followed by a comparison is not enough for this statement. The first milestone is a valid issuer-signed field, a constrained predicate, and a successful contract call. Everything visual comes after that boundary works.

### The Product Case: One Pass Opens A Door, One Edit Closes It

Three tensions matter. First, **a more realistic credential source can make the demo less reliable** if capture, wallet installation, or artifact preparation consumes the timebox. Second, **more privacy does not mean less trust**: hiding a signed field does not remove dependence on the signer. Third, **browser support is not a memory benchmark**: a documented WASM route still needs proof generation on the actual laptop.

The recommended product angle resolves these tensions by making the proof's boundary visible. A trusted issuer signs an eligibility fact, the holder proves one exact condition, and a contract acts without receiving the underlying credential in that flow. The jaw-drop moment is not a spinning cryptographic animation; it is the juxtaposition of a genuine approval, an inspectable minimal receipt, and a convincing forged edit that fails.

**Final selection:** Begin with **R1**, keep its existing signed age predicate, and attempt one valid and several invalid end-to-end cases before committing to the interface. If income-under-limit becomes real within the remaining time, promote the scholarship framing. If it does not, deliver an honest age-eligibility product rather than a falsely labelled scholarship verifier.
