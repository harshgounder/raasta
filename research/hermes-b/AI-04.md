### Ragas And DeepEval Already Cover The Evaluation Skeleton

**Ragas case study.** The repository provides a `ragas quickstart rag_eval` template, prebuilt evaluation metrics and production-aligned test-set generation. Its example accesses `score.reason`, but the examined example also uses `gpt-4o` with an OpenAI API key. It is therefore a strong source of evaluation and dataset workflows, not a verified zero-cost, offline application. Source: https://github.com/vibrantlabsai/ragas. Confidence: high. [1_ten_primary_repositories_identity_licence_and_gaps[0]] [2]

The decision for this team is to reuse its data structures and selected metrics, not to install every optional integration. The failure case is assembling a beautiful upload page whose first click waits on an unavailable paid model. Keep generation separate from scoring, and make the execution mode visible: live local, live hosted, or recorded replay.

**DeepEval case study.** DeepEval describes itself as a Pytest-like LLM evaluation framework. It documents contextual precision, recall and relevancy, answer relevancy and hallucination metrics, synthetic single- and multi-turn datasets, local model execution, and metric reasons. These are unusually close to the statement's requested backend primitives. Source: https://github.com/confident-ai/deepeval. Confidence: high. [1_ten_primary_repositories_identity_licence_and_gaps[1]] [6]

The practical distinction is between the open-source evaluation package and the separate Confident AI platform. Shareable cloud reports and production failure clustering are described separately; do not promise those services are included in a local fork. Use local result objects as the contract for a custom screen rather than making the hackathon depend on a vendor account. Source: https://github.com/confident-ai/deepeval. Confidence: high. ### RAGChecker Explains Failures, But Its Example Is Not Laptop-Sized

**RAGChecker case study.** Its claim-level approach is valuable because one answer can contain several distinct problems. `claim_recall` and `context_precision` describe retrieval; generator-side diagnostics include context utilization, sensitivity to relevant and irrelevant noise, hallucination, self-knowledge and faithfulness. The CLI and Python examples emit metric values and intermediate results. Source: https://github.com/amazon-science/RAGChecker. Confidence: high. [1_ten_primary_repositories_identity_licence_and_gaps[2]] [7]

That vocabulary enables a better story than "accuracy fell": "the correct paragraph was present, but the answer added an unsupported deadline." However, the documented extractor/checker example uses a Bedrock-hosted **Llama3 70B** configuration. There is no verified minimum-memory configuration in the examined material, and no verified ready-made single-screen dashboard. Treat it as a diagnostic engine to adapt or as a schema reference, not as an instant local application. Source: https://github.com/amazon-science/RAGChecker. Confidence: high for the example; medium for the adaptation recommendation. [1_ten_primary_repositories_identity_licence_and_gaps[2]] [7]

### TruLens, Phoenix And Promptfoo Provide Different Visual Starting Points

**TruLens case study.** TruLens describes OpenTelemetry-based tracing, explanatory LLM judges and a leaderboard comparing app versions by scores, latency and cost. It names context relevance, groundedness, NDCG@5 and Groundedness F1. That makes it useful for comparing a deliberately broken retrieval configuration with a corrected one. Source: https://github.com/truera/trulens. Confidence: high. [1_ten_primary_repositories_identity_licence_and_gaps[3]] [3]

The trade-off is product framing. A technical trace is not automatically comprehensible to a non-technical judge. Wrap the trace with a plain-language conclusion and the exact source sentence; keep the trace available under an expandable "show evidence" control. Synthetic question generation and a specific low-memory local judge were not established in the examined source.

**Phoenix case study.** Phoenix describes response and retrieval evaluations, runs locally or in container/cloud deployments, and has Python and TypeScript packages. Its licence is **ELv2**. "Runs locally" does not establish that its full deployment plus a judge model fits **7 GB RAM**. Source: https://github.com/Arize-ai/phoenix. Confidence: high. [1_ten_primary_repositories_identity_licence_and_gaps[4]] [1]

**Promptfoo case study.** Promptfoo supplies the most directly documented visual evaluation surface in this set: a web evaluation matrix and security reports. It is a CLI/library for evaluation and red teaming, with Ollama listed among supported model options. Use it for before/after comparison and adversarial demonstrations; add source-span provenance and independent retrieval scoring yourself. Source: https://github.com/promptfoo/promptfoo. Confidence: high. [1_ten_primary_repositories_identity_licence_and_gaps[6]] [4]

## 5_the_attribution_model_four_independent_questions_not_one_blame_label

RAGChecker's separate retriever and generator diagnostics and DeepEval's separate contextual/output metrics support the central design choice: do not collapse retrieval quality, grounding and correctness into one percentage. Sources: https://github.com/amazon-science/RAGChecker; https://github.com/confident-ai/deepeval. Confidence: high. [5_the_attribution_model_four_independent_questions_not_one_blame_label[0]] [7][5_the_attribution_model_four_independent_questions_not_one_blame_label[1]] [6]

The following is a proposed product specification. Its definitions are explicit so a non-technical judge can follow the result without assuming the system has proven causation.

| Independent question | Proposed measurement | What the screen says | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Did retrieval find the required evidence? | Reference evidence/chunk recall; optional precision and ranking score | "The answer paragraph was missing" | Whether the generator could have answered correctly anyway |
| Did the generator use the supplied context? | Claim-level support/faithfulness assessment | "This sentence has no support in these chunks" | Whether the sentence is false in the outside world |
| Is the answer correct against the approved reference? | Task-specific exact/numerical check or qualified semantic judge | "The stated deadline differs from the reference" | Whether the reference document itself is true |
| What changes under an intervention? | Replace retrieved context with approved context; keep the prompt/model fixed where possible | "With the right paragraph, this run was repaired" | Universal proof that retrieval was the sole cause |

**Takeaway:** unsupported, incorrect and retrieval-deficient are different diagnoses. Allow multiple labels, plus "uncertain" and "not evaluated". Never force every failure into exactly one of "bad retriever" or "hallucination".

**Proposed demonstration case study.** Use a clearly fictional university-policy document with a single explicit application deadline. In the first variant, remove the paragraph containing the deadline; in the second, retain it but supply a recorded answer that invents a different date. This creates two visually distinguishable failures without exposing real personal data or pretending a synthetic answer is a verified real-world fact.

The screen then switches to approved context while holding the rest of the test configuration constant. If a live model is available, show its new output; otherwise show a labelled recorded comparison. The mechanism is visible: one case lacks evidence, the other ignores or adds to available evidence. The lesson is stronger than a single red score, but the wording remains "this intervention changed this result", not "we proved the root cause of all future failures".

### Synthetic References Need A Provenance Chain

Ragas and DeepEval both document synthetic test-data capabilities. They do not remove the need for reference review. Sources: https://github.com/vibrantlabsai/ragas; https://github.com/confident-ai/deepeval. Confidence: high. [5_the_attribution_model_four_independent_questions_not_one_blame_label[2]] [2][5_the_attribution_model_four_independent_questions_not_one_blame_label[1]] [6]

For the proposed fixture, generate **12 cases** as a design target: three straightforward facts, three paraphrases, two questions requiring two source spans, two unanswerable questions, one conflicting-source case and one adversarial instruction embedded in a chunk. These counts are a recommendation, not a benchmark result. Attach the question, reference answer, verbatim support span, source identifier, expected answerability and review state to each case.

Keep some questions manually authored rather than merely reversing a chunk into a question. Include realistic ambiguity and explicitly unanswerable requests. A generated answer should be labelled "draft reference" until checked against its source; once reviewed, it becomes a test reference, not a certificate that the underlying source is factually correct.

**Takeaway:** the attractive tension is that the most diagnostic engine is not the easiest runtime, and the easiest visual shell is not the most complete evaluator. The best short-event product therefore composes a narrow evaluation backend with an intentionally simpler evidence interface.

The non-obvious risk is confusing a correct diagnosis *label* with a causal explanation. A system can miss evidence and still answer correctly, or retrieve the right chunk and still produce an unsupported detail. Independent scoring makes those combinations visible; a controlled intervention strengthens the explanation without pretending to prove more than the test actually shows.

**Final choice:** use **DeepEval plus selected Ragas generation**, with a custom one-screen **Evidence X-Ray + Rescue Button + Failure Receipt**. Keep **Promptfoo** as the alternative comparison-first path. Do not select a commercial API, pretrained judge or heavyweight observability deployment until its current price, licence and actual memory footprint are established.
